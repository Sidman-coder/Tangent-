## Synthesis
- **Highest friction point:** Steps 8-9. A visible "Stop and plan" led to a second question in the chat instead of a plan. The first panel asked "When is this?" although her sentence gave Dec 17, and its skip default ("about 2 weeks") contradicts her date. Two intake turns came before any value, which breaks her limit of two questions. "How do I prepare..." never produced advice text. Step 4 is a close second: the first capture (about 30 s) produced one step and dropped the Precalc quiz.
- **Highest emotional friction point:** Step 4, then step 7. In step 4 she saw her typed days overridden ("No day given", though she gave Friday and Tuesday) and 6 stored facts she never typed. In step 7 the planning page had closed on its own and the screen showed "9 past due" and "Still up, Sid?", which read as guilt.
- **Final decision:** Continues and finishes. She confirmed at step 10 and treated step 11 as the end. She reached the goal despite near-abandonment at steps 6, 8 and 9. The 5-step plan matched her limits (debate Tue 3:30-5:00, nothing after 11 pm), and that was enough to keep her.
- **Emotional summary:** "I opened the app and got two popups before I could do anything. I typed my two tests and watched 'Placing...' for ages, and it gave me one step on the wrong day with a list of things about me I never told it. I hit Confirm just to get something done. Then the screen closed on me, and I had to type my question all over again. When it asked 'When is this?' about a date I'd already written, I almost left. I hit 'Stop and plan' and it asked me another question, so I told it flat out: no more questions, and nothing in debate or after 11. That worked. The plan looked like my week, and I saw 'Added 5 steps' and it felt real. But nobody ever told me how to prepare for the final, and my Friday exam and the Precalc quiz are still not there."
- **Detected risks:**
  - Students with a low question tolerance (this profile quits after two) leave at the intake panel or the chat question.
  - Stacked first-load modals and a crowded Home delay the first capture.
  - A ~30 s wait with only "Placing..." and a draft that drops part of the input (Precalc quiz, the stated days) make users distrust capture.
  - A screen that closes by itself loses the user's chat context.
  - A user who assumes "Confirm" saves everything may believe all her tests are on the calendar.
  - Exam prep filed under "Personal" misleads her about what Tangent understood.
  - "9 past due" and "Still up, Sid?" read as guilt for a user who gets irritated by red numbers and guilt wording.
  - A long, generic "What I assumed" list (idea stage, build and write-up milestones, Dec 15 goal) reads as if it describes some other user's project.
- **Structural insight:** The flow works only when the user volunteers hard constraints in free text (as Maya did at step 9). The capture-to-plan path does not carry the dates and items she already typed, and "Stop" does not mean "plan now". Tangent asks for what it already has and still drops items she gave. The product's rule "ask only what changes the plan, always with a Skip and a Stop that never block" is broken by this run: Stop led to another question, and Skip's default conflicts with the stated date.
- **Fix this first:** Make "Stop and plan" produce the plan (or an answer to the prep question) immediately, with no further question. Also parse the dates and items already in the sentence ("Thu Dec 17", "Tuesday 3:30-5:00", "Friday exam", "Precalc quiz") so they are never asked for again and never replaced by a default.

# Tangent-specific lens

### Context
Mixed. At step 4 Tangent ignored her typed days ("No day given" although she typed Friday and Tuesday), dropped the Precalc quiz, and showed 6 stored facts she never typed. At step 8 it asked for a date she had already given, and its default ("about 2 weeks") contradicted Dec 17. Only after she restated debate Tuesday 3:30-5:00 and "after 11pm" in the step 9 chat did the step 10 plan respect them. Its "What I used about you" then held 3 items, all matching what she said. The "What I assumed" list still carried lines that didn't fit her ("Starting from the idea stage", "Standard milestones: idea, research, build, write-up, practice", "Your goal runs to Tue, Dec 15").

### Different from generic apps
The plan format is a real difference. Each step has a "Why" and a "You'll have ..." outcome, and "Nothing is on your calendar until you confirm" is stated, followed by an explicit "Added 5 steps" and Undo. A task app like Todoist would just record her text, and ChatGPT would simply answer the prep question. Tangent did neither well here. The prep question never got advice text, and capture lost an item. The planning is closer to a scheduler than a chat, but the cost was long waits (about 30 s, under 25 s) and several questions first.

### Follow-through
After the first Confirm, only 1 of 2 captured items was on the calendar, and the planning page closed on its own about 40-60 s later with no way back to the reply box. Home then showed "1 left today" and the step under "Personal". After the second Confirm, step 11 gave a clear "Added 5 steps to your calendar." with Undo. The run did not reach the Calendar tab, so it did not check that the steps really appear there. Nothing in this run showed a check-in, daily bubble or round review, so I cannot say anything about those.

### Trust
Trust rose and fell. It dropped at step 4 (stored facts she never typed and a changed day), at step 6-7 (the page closed and the chat was lost), and at step 8 ("it didn't read me"). It rose at step 10 because no step landed in debate or after 11 pm, and "What I used about you" matched what she said. She could see what was used and could Undo. The "Nothing is on your calendar until you confirm" line is clear, but the first draft's unexplained account facts and the long assumption list remained a weak point.
