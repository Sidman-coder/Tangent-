## Synthesis
- **Highest friction point:** Steps 9–11, from the saved plan through Mark done to the missing check-in. These steps ended the run. The only completion control on the sheet is the sticky "Mark done", and it belongs to the parent "AP Chem final" task, not to plan step 1. It completed the wrong item (orchestrator-observed: "Confirm final scope and list units" stayed not done), gave no toast or step name, and pulled focus to an "Undo" button. After that, the "how did it go" moment the task asks for doesn't exist on this surface. The slot shows a "Help Tangent plan better 1 of 3" profile question instead. Close second: steps 2–4, where the capture invented a date and the Ask box failed to parse a clear, complete instruction.
- **Highest emotional friction point:** Step 4, "What did you mean?". Maya had given Tangent everything (date, debate slot, 11pm cutoff) in one message and felt "honestly kind of insulted". It was the first time she named a fallback ("I'll go back to Google Calendar"). Step 6 is a sharp secondary spike: the plan placed a session in debate, her stated instant trust-breaker ("are you serious, I literally said debate").
- **Final decision:** She abandons at step 11 (of a 15-step cap). She considers the plan saved (from step 9) but the task unfinished: she doesn't know what was marked done and found no way to tell Tangent how it went.
- **Emotional summary:** "I came in thinking I could just type 'AP Chem final' and it would know. Instead it told me my final was tomorrow, and for a second my stomach actually dropped. I gave it the real date, my debate time and my cutoff all in one go, and it asked what I meant. That's when I started planning to just do it in Google Calendar. When the five sessions with real units finally showed up, I liked them, honestly. It looked like a plan someone who knows chem would write. Then I saw Tuesday at 4:30 and felt dumb for trusting it, because I'd said debate twice. I fixed it myself, hit Confirm, nothing happened, and I was just jabbing buttons. 'Added' was the first time I relaxed. Then I hit Mark done and got a little label change and a box around Undo, so I couldn't even tell what I'd finished. And instead of asking how it went, it wanted to know how many hours a week I have. It still doesn't know my final is December 17, and I don't know what it knows about me or where that video thing came from. I'm not finishing this tonight."
- **Detected risks:**
  - Students who type a bare event name ("AP Chem final") get an invented date ("tomorrow 3:30 PM") presented as fact. Some will panic, and others will miss that the date is wrong.
  - A message that clearly states a constraint, sent through "Stuck? Ask…", can get "What did you mean?". Fast, impatient users read that as the app not listening and leave before seeing any value.
  - Stated constraints (debate Tue 3:30–5) and the real deadline (Dec 17) are not reflected in the plan. The plan ends at Nov 28–29, so a student could believe they are prepared when weeks before the final have no study scheduled.
  - "Mark done" on a parent task that has a plan under it completes the whole goal, not the first step. Users can close out an exam weeks early without knowing it, and the plan's real step 1 stays open.
  - A Confirm tap swallowed after editing a field looks like a broken save. Users may leave believing nothing saved, or tap again and duplicate work.
  - Too many questions before and after value: a promised question that wasn't there, "What did you mean?", the units question, then "Help Tangent plan better 1 of 3". That exceeds this persona's two-question tolerance and makes questions feel like homework.
  - "Used:" shows stored context with no visible source or control. When an item is wrong or unrecognised, users conclude the app holds data about them they can't see or fix.
  - The persona assumed Canvas was connected without connecting it. Nothing on the path showed that Tangent had no school data, which fed the "it should already know" expectation.
- **Structural insight:** Capture and planning work once the student pushes past the chat. The units-specific 5-session draft is a real differentiator, and "skip and plan it anyway" is a good stoppable exit. The flow breaks at the two ends of the loop. At the start, Tangent fills in what it doesn't know (the date) instead of asking the one question that would change the plan, and it doesn't carry the user's stated constraints into the plan. At the end, the "done" part of capture, plan, done is attached to the wrong object (the parent goal, not the plan's step) and has no follow-through. The sheet treats the original task and the new plan as one item, so every button means something different from what the student expects.
- **Tangent-specific checks:**
  - **Context (did Tangent use what she said?):** Mostly no. She wrote: "the final is Thu Dec 17 not tomorrow. spread study sessions over the weeks before it, not tue 3:30-5 (debate) and nothing after 11pm", and Tangent replied "What did you mean?". The eventual draft cited stored account context ("You do your best work late at night", "2 hours on a school night") but not debate or Dec 17. It scheduled into debate and stopped at Nov 29 (orchestrator-observed). The 11pm cutoff was respected, but nothing showed whether that was deliberate. She did get unit-level content (stoichiometry, equilibrium, acids), which matches the subject.
  - **Different from generic apps (Todoist/Notion/ChatGPT):** Partly. The units-based study breakdown with real sessions on a calendar is something Todoist or Google Calendar wouldn't produce, and it was the one moment she felt value. But she had to move a session by hand as she would in Google Calendar, and a general chatbot would likely have parsed her first message. Her own fallback was "go back to Google Calendar and do it myself."
  - **Follow-through (check-ins, round review, daily bubble):** Failed on this path. Marking done produced no check-in, no "how did it go", no toast and no next step, only a profile question ("How many hours a week…"). Round review and the daily bubble were not reached in this run, so they were not evaluated.
  - **Trust (what is stored, can she control it?):** The "Used:" line is the right idea and partly meets her need to know what is stored about her. But it showed an item she didn't recognise (the video-upload note; the content is a test artifact, but the surfacing is real product behaviour) with no source and no visible way to remove or correct it. She reacted with "I don't know where that came from" and carried that distrust to the end. No control over stored context was visible during the run.
- **Fix this first:** On the task sheet, once a plan has been saved (the "Added … · 5 sessions" state), change the sticky "Mark done" so it acts on the next plan step, not the parent task. Label it with the step ("Mark step 1 done: Confirm final scope and list units"). On tap, show a confirmation naming that step, then in the same slot show a one-tap "How did it go?" check-in (for example "Easy / OK / Rough" plus an optional note, with Skip). Show it instead of the "Help Tangent plan better" question, not alongside it.
