# Simulation report — Sam Patel (sam-patel-t5d), 9th-grade freshman who procrastinates, end user

Run: overnight 2 Phase 8 (round 2), Playwright, 390x844, production build on push-v1.

**Task:** Add something from your week that you need to get ready for, get Tangent to plan it into your calendar, save the plan, then mark the first step done and tell Tangent how it went.
**Result:** Abandoned. Sam closed the app at step 7 and counted the task as unresolved. He did every action the task asked for: capture, plan, Confirm ("Saved 5 steps to your calendar"), tick the first step, and check in with "Okay" and Save. Nothing confirmed the check-in after Save, and his profile rule says "Marks the task unresolved if there is no final confirmation." The step cap was not the cause: he used 7 of 15 steps.
**Steps:** 7 · **Total estimated time:** 82 s

> Test-artifact note: this run used a shared test account that still holds earlier personas' data: the "Good morning, Leo." greeting, AP Chem, Python/Codeforces, Environmental Club, bakery shifts, video uploads, the ED II essay, a 31-item list and 7 notifications. Sam's "this isn't mine" reactions to that content come from the test setup and are not treated as product defects. Two product behaviours are still judged here: (a) the new capture was added to an open draft plan ("ED II Supplemental Essay Draft") instead of starting its own plan, and (b) the "Draft plan" pill stayed on screen after Confirm.

## Synthesis
- **Highest friction point:** **Step 7, Calendar after the check-in Save.** It was the only Low-clarity step, and it decided the outcome. The card disappeared with no acknowledgement, and the "Draft plan" pill came back at the same moment, saying "not finished." Every action had worked, but nothing told Sam so. The same pill also caused doubt at steps 2, 3 and 4, so this is a recurring problem, not a one-off.
- **Highest emotional friction point:** **Step 4, Calendar Agenda.** It was the longest step (25s). Sam's "stomach drops", he nearly closed the app, and he marked the step done by guessing ("hoping I don't break anything"). Caveat: much of the volume was other personas' data. The unlabelled circle and having to scroll to find his step after "View in Calendar" are real product issues. The emotional low that actually ended the run was step 7.
- **Final decision:** Closed the app at step 7 with the task marked unresolved. He completed all task actions, so the loss came from missing feedback, not missing capability.
- **Emotional summary:** I almost closed it on the first screen, it was so packed. The type-anything box was the one thing I got, so I tried it. It made me a plan that was too wordy, but the first step was just "pick a prompt," and that's what kept me going. When it said saved and showed me Monday 4:30, I started to trust it a little. Then that Draft plan bubble kept sitting there like it hadn't really saved. In the calendar I had to dig, and I tapped a circle hoping it was the right thing. When it asked how it went with three buttons, I thought, okay, this is actually nice. Then one tap turned into a form, I hit Save, and it just vanished. No "got it," and the draft thing was back. I did everything it asked and still don't know if it counted, so I'm out.
- **Calibration checks (Tangent-specific):**
  - **Context (did Tangent use what Sam said?):** Partly. It turned "english essay due friday" into a concrete first step ("Pick the English essay prompt") scheduled before Friday, and Sam liked that it "remembered" the prompt wasn't picked yet. But the capture went into the already-open ED II draft, so Sam got "a big essay I never mentioned." Some Because reasons cited facts from the shared account's memory. A real single user might see this less, but adding a new capture to an unrelated open draft is a product behaviour and would still happen whenever a draft is open. It is unclear whether every Because fact would have been Sam's own.
  - **Different from generic apps (Todoist/Notion/ChatGPT):** Yes, where it worked. One typed line became a dated, scheduled step plan that went onto the calendar after an explicit Confirm. Ticking a step brought up a one-tap check-in (Nailed it/Okay/Struggled). Todoist would have stored one task and ChatGPT would have produced text with no calendar. The silent Save undercut the most distinctive part of that loop.
  - **Follow-through:** The check-in fired immediately on ticking a step, which earned the run's only High clarity. Tangent gave no reaction to the check-in, so it felt one-way. Round review and the daily bubble were not reached in this run.
  - **Trust (what is stored, can he control it):** "Nothing goes on my calendar until I confirm" built trust, and so did the small first step. The persistent "Draft plan" pill eroded trust in the save at steps 3, 4 and 7. Sam never saw anything telling him whether it's safe to tell Tangent things, or what is kept. That is one of the profile's required pieces of explicit information, and it went unmet. No privacy controls were seen in this run.
- **Detected risks:**
  - Students with low tolerance for ambiguity will treat a silent save as a failed save and leave, even when it succeeded. They may then redo the check-in or never return.
  - A "Draft plan" pill that stays after Confirm makes the user think the plan isn't saved, or that there is unfinished work. For an avoidant user, that is a reason to stop.
  - Adding a new capture to an unrelated open draft gives the user steps they didn't ask for ("extra essay"). Wrong steps may end up on their calendar if they Confirm without reading, as Sam did.
  - The unlabelled completion circle depends on guessing. Users who don't guess never reach the check-in.
  - Long multi-card plans with Why/Because on every card ask more reading than short-attention users will do. They confirm without reading, so the reasons don't help them.
  - A dense Home or Agenda (31 items, 7 notifications) can trigger the "long list, close it" response before the first capture. In this run that was inflated by shared data, but a real user with a backlog would hit the same pattern.
  - A check-in that grows from one tap into a form adds unexpected effort at the end of the flow.
- **Structural insight:** The capture, plan, done loop works. Each step did what it should, and the small first step plus the one-tap check-in are what kept a near-abandoning user going. The run was lost at the transitions: after Confirm (the pill still says Draft) and after the check-in Save (nothing at all). For low-trust users, every state change needs visible acknowledgement and a clear next step. Here the end of the loop gave no feedback.
- **Fix this first:** After the check-in Save in Calendar, replace the vanishing card with a visible confirmation that stays for about 4 seconds and is announced in the alert/status region. For example: "Got it, logged as Okay. Next: <step 2 title>, <day, time>." This gives Sam the final confirmation his profile requires and his next action in one line.
