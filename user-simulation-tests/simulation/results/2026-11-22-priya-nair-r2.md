## Synthesis
- **Highest friction point:** The plan proposal, steps 2-3. Tangent placed "Outline essay" inside a shift she had stated in the same sentence (Wed 4-9pm), with a Why line that cited the shift. When she asked it to move the step, it regenerated the plan, kept the same Wed 6:30 PM slot, rewrote the Why line to say the step "comes after your 4-9pm hours", and sent no chat reply. This cost 135 s and produced the run's first Low clarity. She had to fix it by hand in Edit (steps 4-5), and the stale Why line survived her edit.
- **Highest emotional friction point:** The end of the loop, steps 7-11, peaking at step 11. From step 7 on, the leftover "Draft plan · 5 steps" pill with a Discard × contradicted "Saved 5 steps" and the sidebar's progress count ("my stomach drops… did I save it or not?"). After the feedback Save, the popover closed with no acknowledgement. Together these turned the relief of step 6 into "I don't trust either one now."
- **Final decision:** She stopped at step 11 and marked the task unresolved under her "no final confirmation" rule. Every task action was completed: capture, plan, edit, Confirm (5 steps saved), step 1 marked done ("1 of 5 done"), and feedback submitted (Okay / More time / note / Save). The run ended on a perception and confirmation failure, not a functional dead end.
- **Emotional summary:** I came in tense because the screen was full of someone else's week, but typing my essay and my hours felt easy. When the plan came back with reasons under every step I was honestly impressed, until I saw the outline sitting in my Wednesday shift with "your shift ends at 9pm" written right under it. I asked it to move the step, and it rewrote the excuse to say it had, but the time hadn't changed. That's what got me, because it sounded like it was listening. I fixed it myself and confirmed because the dates were finally right, not because I believed its reasons. "Saved 5 steps" felt good for about a minute. Then the "Draft plan" thing in the corner made me doubt it, and it never went away. I found Done, told it the step took more time, wrote a careful note because I didn't know who reads it, pressed Save, and nothing happened. I did everything it asked and I still can't tell you whether any of it counted.
- **Tangent check — Context (did Tangent use what she said?):** Partly. It used her weekend hours (steps after Saturday's 2 PM end, Monday treated as free), and the step 8 BECAUSE line quoted her own words, which she valued. It failed on the most important constraint: the Wednesday shift was broken twice, and the second time with a Why line claiming it had been respected. Her follow-up reply got no answer, and her shifts were never stored as busy time, so in step 7 she couldn't check whether Saturday 3 PM was too tight after work. Her persona-level wrong assumption ("the plan knows about her bakery shifts") was half right, which is worse than either outcome because she couldn't tell which half to trust.
- **Tangent check — Different from generic apps:** Yes, clearly. One typed sentence became a dated 5-step plan with a reason per step, a "nothing is on your calendar until you confirm" gate, Undo, and a check-in right after Done that asks how long the step took. Todoist, Notion or ChatGPT would not do this without manual work. The difference only helps if the reasons are true. Here a false Why line weakened the feature that sets Tangent apart.
- **Tangent check — Follow-through:** The check-in fired right after Done and asked the right question ("How long it took", which she called "the part that actually matters"). But nothing closed the loop: there was no acknowledgement and no visible sign that "More time" would change the remaining steps. The calendar block also didn't clearly show the done state. The run never reached the round review or the daily bubble.
- **Tangent check — Trust:** Mixed. The confirm gate and Undo built trust. Trust was lost when the Canvas card didn't say what connecting reads (step 1), the note box didn't say where the note is stored or who sees it (step 10, she deliberately kept it vague), the Why line was false (step 3), and the "Draft plan" pill contradicted the saved state (steps 7-11). She never visited Settings, so the run did not test what is stored or how to control it.
- **Test-setup caveat:** This was a shared test account. The greeting and avatar said "Leo", the feed carried earlier personas' items, and several "Because" lines cited other personas' remembered facts (late-night work, putting things off, a virtual contest, a video upload, 40-minute sessions before a final). Those are account artifacts, not product behaviour, but they still lowered her trust in the reasons during this run. These are treated as product behaviour: the Wednesday shift placement and the false Why lines (steps 2-3), the stale Why line after her edit, the leftover "Draft plan" pill after Confirm, and the missing confirmation after the feedback Save. The "ECs" category chip on an essay step (step 8) is a minor mislabel she didn't react to.
- **Detected risks:**
  - A less careful student confirms the first proposal and ends up with an essay block in the middle of a work shift. The Why line ("your shift ends at 9pm") makes the clash look intentional, so they may not notice.
  - Why lines can say something the placed time contradicts. For a user who checks the reason for every step, one false reason undermines all of them.
  - Corrections sent in "Reply to Tangent" can be silently ignored and get no response. Users learn that talking to Tangent doesn't work, which runs against "continue in task/project chats".
  - The leftover "Draft plan · 5 steps" pill with a Discard × after a successful save invites users to wonder whether they saved, or to tap Discard on a plan they already committed.
  - A Save with no acknowledgement makes the check-in feel like it went nowhere, so users may skip or rush future check-ins and Tangent loses its follow-through signal.
  - Stated recurring commitments (shifts) are not shown as busy time, so users can't check the plan against their real week on the calendar.
  - The done control is only inside the calendar block dialog, not on the saved-plan step list. Under her rule, "abandons if the next action is not visible", she nearly abandoned at step 7.
  - Free-text fields don't say where the text is stored, so privacy-minded users give vague input and the context Tangent learns from is weaker.
- **Structural insight:** Planning was strong, and the run failed at two seams. First, between what she said and what got scheduled: a stated hard constraint was handled as soft input, and the explanation layer was generated separately from the placement, so it could claim compliance that didn't exist. Second, at the end of the flow: neither Confirm nor the feedback Save left a single, unambiguous "this is saved" state, because the draft UI outlived the committed plan and the check-in closed silently. For a sceptical user, every claim on screen (Why lines, "Draft" labels, success states) has to match what actually happened, or she reads the whole screen as unreliable.
- **Fix this first:** Treat unavailable hours stated in a capture or a reply ("I work Wed 4-9pm") as hard blocks during placement, so no step can be placed inside them. Before the proposal renders, check each step's Why line against its placed time, and drop or rewrite any reason that the time contradicts (for example, "after your 4-9pm hours" on a 6:30 PM Wednesday slot).
