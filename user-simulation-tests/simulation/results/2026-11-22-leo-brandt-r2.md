## Different from generic apps / Follow-through / Trust
- **Versus Notion/ChatGPT:**
  - Tangent did better than generic advice on the first plan, because it used his stored constraints.
  - It did worse than Notion on predictable editing: the time field only saves when you click out of it, and Leo saw his typed text mangled.
  - Leo's last thought was "a fancier version of the same thing".
- **Follow-through:** Ticking a step done triggered no check-in. The report-back worked only because Leo went looking for the Ask box himself. The reply promised resizing that didn't visibly happen.
- **Trust:**
  - Leo could see what was stored, via the Used list.
  - He never saw whether Tangent could edit his goal later. The goal box just held his raw chat message.
  - He never saw where his channel work lives relative to this plan.

## Synthesis
- **Highest friction point:** The proposal's edit and confirm controls (steps 4–8, about 169 s, nearly half the run).
  - The reply was hidden under the sticky Done button.
  - Two generated steps were dated after the test.
  - The summary bullets contradicted the dates ("Spread … to Sat, Nov 28" next to a Sun Nov 29 step and "goal runs to Sun, Nov 29").
  - A typed time was dropped because the field only saves when you click out of it.
  - Confirm, Done editing, sticky Done, Clear and "Enter to confirm" all compete as the save action.
- **Highest emotional friction point:** Step 13, the report-back. Leo gave two concrete, actionable facts and got a generic "Thanks for telling me" with no visible change. That hit his core trust trigger, generic AI, at the moment that should show what makes Tangent different. Runner-up is step 10, where "Later" erased the whole Added card, including Undo. That was the only Low-clarity step.
- **Final decision:** Continues to the end. He accepts "Logged how … went" at step 13 as the final confirmation and calls the task done, "though not in a satisfying way".
- **Emotional summary:** I started out curious. It looked clean, almost like Notion, and it already knew about my Sunday upload. Then it chopped my sentence into garbage, and a card promised a question that wasn't there, so I was already halfway out the door by step three. The chat plan pulled me back, because it actually knew my channel, my 45-minute focus and my school-night limit. That's the moment I thought this might be different. Then I spent ages fighting it: steps after my test, a time I never typed, buttons that all sounded like "save", and a "Later" that ate the whole confirmation card. The project page finally made sense and ticking the step felt good. But when I told it something real, that logs are shaky and Thursday is my edit night, it said thanks and left everything where it was. It remembered what I'd told it before, but it ignored what I told it just now.
- **Detected risks:**
  - Mangled capture fragments ("Get ready", "edit 's video") clutter Today and teach students not to trust capture with run-on sentences, which is how this persona naturally types.
  - "Tangent has a question" rows that contain no question break the promise behind the entry point. Students may never discover that the "Ask about" box is how planning starts.
  - Plans that schedule prep after the deadline undermine trust in every generated date. A less careful student would Confirm and study after the test.
  - Time edits silently dropped when the user clicks Done editing straight from a field lead to saved plans with wrong times.
  - Clicking "Later" on a follow-up removes Undo and the session list. The user loses the safety net right after committing.
  - A student may press the dialog's big Done thinking it saves the plan, and mark the test itself done instead.
  - No check-in after a step is ticked, and a check-in that changes nothing, means the "tell Tangent how it went" loop feels pointless. Burst workers like Leo will stop reporting back.
  - Channel work is never connected to school prep. The persona's core goal (uploads without grades dropping) is not served by one combined plan.
  - Leo's required information was only partly met. Why the plan fits him: yes, via the Used list. Where steps sit on the calendar: only after going to the project page. Whether the AI can edit his goal later: never answered.
- **Structural insight:** Tangent's strength, using stored context in plan generation, only shows up in the middle of the flow. Both ends of the loop are weak:
  - **Entry:** capture parsing and the empty "question" card.
  - **Exit:** no check-in after a step is done, and an acknowledgement that doesn't act on new facts.

  The proposal, the single place where trust is won, also carries too many overlapping actions and date inconsistencies. As a result, the differentiator (planning that adapts to him) is promised ("It helps size the next steps") but never visibly delivered within the session.
- **Fix this first:** When a student reports back on a step, have Tangent act on the concrete facts and show what changed in the reply. Here, that means seeing "logs are still shaky" and "edit Sunday's video Thursday night", then proposing to move "Learn log rules and change of base" off Thu 6:30 PM and give logs more time. The reply would list the before and after time with an Undo, instead of "Thanks for telling me. It helps size the next steps."
