## 3. New issues (top 15)

1. **The product cannot be demonstrated today** (fatal for a demo). Every AI feature, which is the entire pitch, returns an error while the Anthropic credit balance is empty (DECISIONS D6, LAUNCH-REVIEW B2: 96 of 146 calls failed with HTTP 400). Tonight's polish is moot in front of an investor if the first sentence typed yields "I couldn't do that right now". Cheapest: top up, then re-run the six persona runs and the eval before anyone sees it.
2. **Round 2 produced no evidence on the engine** (significant). Chat, plan, capture, check-in and cost were not re-run. 980 unit tests and screenshots of empty-ish screens are not the same as "the plan is better". The investor claim "context engine" rests on a 39/64 before-eval at about $0.27 and nothing since. A reader of the round-2 report should not infer improvement in the engine.
3. **Still no parent-readable trust layer, and a consent gap is now documented** (fatal for distribution). No `/privacy`, `/terms`, `/parents` route exists (LAUNCH-REVIEW B1); the sign-in page has no links (`heur-signin`, heuristic F11). Student text goes to Anthropic and audio to Deepgram, with no distinct consent step (LAUNCH-REVIEW section 3, 5.1.2). Observed habits are learned but not shown or erased by Forget everything (CONTEXT-REVIEW section 6). For a product for 13 to 17 year olds sold through parents, this is the gate on distribution, not a polish item.
4. **The edge is invisible on the screens a viewer sees first** (significant). Home after has no trace of context ("because you told me..."); the sign-in page headline "Your week, planned around you" still omits "follows up" (the product's own tagline, per BASELINE), and "listens" and "built from what it already knows about you" are unchanged. The one place context shows is the Settings map, which is the wrong place for a pitch.
5. **The follow-up half is still a roadmap, not a product** (significant). The only retention surface shipped is a "Next:" line. The 4.5 post-event reviews are a plan, an unapplied migration and a mockup with sample data. That is a sensible idea (reflection after an event, free fixed questions, cost about $0.04 a student a month if the estimate holds; the Deepgram rate is flagged unconfirmed), but it also adds scope beside the stated guardrail "build only capture, plan, done". Not a product until a two-day account shows a check-in changing the next step.
6. **Payments and unit economics are blank** (significant, investor lens). Plan brief is about 65% of AI cost at roughly $0.011 a call, but cost per active student is "argued, not measured" (CONTEXT-REVIEW section 5), `ai_usage` does not record the task, and the pricing path is undecided (parent pays vs student, IAP vs web; LAUNCH-REVIEW section 3). I will not guess numbers. An investor will ask "what does a student cost per month and who pays", and the repo has no answer.
7. **Possible data bug left unresolved** (significant, unknown). Round 1: Leo's Confirm said "Added 6 steps" but created no project. After shots show the same account with no channel project in Projects or the sidebar (`projects-1440-dark` shows School only). The fix list called for investigating; PROGRESS does not say it was.
8. **Capture is still a second home** (minor to significant). `capture-390-dark` after says "To do, 0 things" while Home on the same account lists a Sunday item under Coming up. Round-1 baseline flagged two homes with different counts; no change is recorded. The mic-first page is the phone's front door.
9. **No offline behavior; typed capture is lost** (significant). No service worker; a capture typed offline is not queued (LAUNCH-REVIEW S1). For a product whose first promise is "Say it, it's saved", a lost sentence is a broken promise. The error text is now friendly; the loss is not fixed.
10. **The product still echoes the user's own sentence as the task title** (minor to significant). `calendar-1440-dark` after: three Sundays all titled with the full onboarding sentence. That is the "plan reads like my own words handed back" note from the Leo run, and a recurring item is a weak demonstration of planning. Title can now be edited, but the default is not shortened.
11. **"You, mapped" remains the showpiece and still looks off-brand** (minor). Cream glow against a dark app, ~8 px labels, one label cut off at the right edge at 390, repeated counts three times (heuristic F09). It is the one screen that tells the context-engine story, so style drift here costs more than elsewhere.
12. **Destructive wording and confirmations unverified** (minor). Forget, Clear, Delete, Remove are used inconsistently (heuristic F05); confirmation dialogs were not captured in round 2 (F06), so a parent cannot be told they exist. The round-1 type-DELETE flow is a strength; it was not re-shown.
13. **Naming and filters still diverge** (minor). "Tangents" is jargon and sits under Projects (F02); Home filters "Personal" while Calendar says "Short-term" (F03); icon-only mic, send and dismiss controls (F07); different Done labels (F04). Individually small, together they undercut "scannable in 3 seconds".
14. **Account-state confound makes the before/after comparison weaker than it looks** (minor, a process note). Different persona, different data, one evaluator, seven screens at one size (heuristic report). The drop from 17 findings to 11 should not be read as a 35 percent improvement.
15. **Security and abuse basics for a minors' product are not set** (significant if opened up, minor in a closed pilot). No security headers, no app-level rate limits, uneven input validation (LAUNCH-REVIEW S4, S5, S6); RLS is claimed on all 26 tables from migration files only, not the live database. Acceptable for an invited pilot with an allowlist; not for an open launch.

## 4. Riskiest assumption now, and the cheapest test

**Riskiest assumption (sharpened, not replaced).** That a student's first typed sentence, plus what Tangent already knows, becomes a plan the student trusts and confirms without help, comes back for on day 2, and answers a check-in. Round 1 listed four weak links: intake friction, silent confirm, an unseen follow-up loop, and a parent who cannot read anything. Round 2 moved the first two from "visibly broken" to "plausibly fixed in code, not re-observed", left the third and fourth unchanged, and added a precondition: the AI path has to be switched on and affordable before any of this can be tested. So the assumption has changed in one respect: it is no longer mainly "will the shell frustrate them" but "does the engine, running, produce a plan and a follow-up that changes behavior, at a cost per student someone will pay". The answer from round 1 (a 10-student, 3-day concierge pilot) still stands as the cheapest test; what changed is that two cheaper steps now precede it.

**Cheapest test, in order.**
1. **Gate (hours, cents to a few dollars):** top up credits, re-run the six persona simulations and `npm run eval:pipeline` once (about $0.30 per the CONTEXT-REVIEW estimate, within the $3 budget). This confirms or refutes C02 to C05 and C09 live, and it measures cost per task. If the same five of six plans still respect constraints, proceed. If not, the problem is the engine, not the pilot.
2. **Pilot (as in round 1):** 10 students, 3 days, concierge follow-ups. Three numbers only: first-session confirm without help; unprompted open on day 2; answered check-in. Add the parent-consent line. Add a fourth number now that cost is knowable: AI cost per student per day, from `ai_usage`.
3. **Pre-condition for any outside pilot:** publish a one-page privacy and "for parents" page (C06, a route-guard change), since a parent will otherwise stop the test before the student does.

**What would change this view.** A recorded two-day run where a check-in answer visibly changes the next day's steps would move differentiation up several points. A plan-quality regression in the re-run eval would move it down. Real pilot day-2 return below half would say the follow-up is not yet a retention engine.

## 5. What is good and should not be broken

- The plan card pattern (Why per step, "What I used about you", draft until confirm, Undo), unchanged and still the core differentiator; round 2 did not touch its structure.
- Honesty fixes: names a data source only if read, no praise while steps remain, friendly errors instead of raw upstream JSON (verified by the live re-check in PROGRESS, not by a screenshot). These are cheap trust wins and they matter to a skeptical parent or investor.
- Home after: a neutral, quiet, one-next-action screen. Keep it that quiet.
- Settings trust surfaces (What Tangent knows with per-fact Forget, Your data in plain language).
- Cost discipline: one place assembles context, utility calls get no student data, no third-party analytics or ad SDKs (CONTEXT-REVIEW sections 1 and 7, LAUNCH-REVIEW section 2).
